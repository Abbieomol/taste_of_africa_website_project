Egusi Soup

Melon Seed Soup with Greens

A rich, nutty Nigerian soup thickened with ground melon seeds, palm oil and leafy greens.

  • Total time1 hr 10 min
  • Serves6
  • DifficultyMedium

The story behind the dish

Egusi soup is one of the most loved soups in Nigeria and across West Africa, from Ghana to Cameroon. Its signature ingredient is egusi — protein-rich seeds from a wild melon, dried and ground into a pale powder that thickens the soup into soft, nutty curds.

The flavour is built on red palm oil, ground crayfish, smoked fish and fiery scotch bonnet peppers. Egusi is eaten with a "swallow" such as pounded yam, eba or fufu — pinched off by hand and dipped into the soup.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups ground egusi (melon seeds)
  • ½ cup red palm oil
  • 500 g assorted meat (beef, goat or chicken), boiled
  • 200 g smoked fish or stockfish, deboned
  • 3 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 2 scotch bonnet peppers, blended
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 2 seasoning cubes
  • 3 cups meat stock
  • 2 cups ugu (pumpkin leaves) or spinach, chopped
  • Salt to taste

Serve with: Pounded yam, eba (garri), fufu or semovita.

Cooking Instructions

Tap each step to expand it.

1 Make the egusi paste
  1. Mix the ground egusi with a little water and half the onion to form a thick paste, like wet sand.
2 Fry in palm oil
  1. Heat the palm oil gently (don't let it smoke) and fry the remaining onion.
  2. Drop in the egusi paste in small lumps and leave it alone for 5 minutes so it sets into curds.
3 Build the soup
  1. Add the stock, meat, smoked fish, crayfish, pepper and seasoning cubes.
  2. Stir gently and simmer for 15–20 minutes, adding more stock if it gets too thick.
4 Finish with greens
  1. Stir in the ugu or spinach and cook for 3–5 minutes more.
  2. Taste, adjust salt and serve hot with pounded yam, eba or fufu.
