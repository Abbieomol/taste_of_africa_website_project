Kuku Kienyeji

Free-Range Chicken Stew

Slow-cooked village chicken in a simple tomato gravy — Kenya's most treasured Sunday and holiday meal.

  • Total time2 hr 30 min
  • Serves4
  • DifficultyMedium

The story behind the dish

Kienyeji means "local" or "traditional", and kuku kienyeji is the free-range village chicken that roams Kenyan homesteads. It is leaner and firmer than farm broilers, with a deep flavour that needs only a few ingredients to shine.

Among the Luhya of Western Kenya, serving chicken is a mark of respect for an important guest. The secret is slow boiling — often 1½ to 2 hours — before the meat is fried down with onions and tomatoes into a rich stew.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole kienyeji chicken (about 1.2 kg), cut into pieces
  • 4 cups water for boiling
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 4 tomatoes, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 tbsp ginger, grated
  • 1 green capsicum, diced
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • A bunch of dhania (coriander), chopped

Serve with: Ugali, chapati, managu or saga (traditional greens).

Cooking Instructions

Tap each step to expand it.

1 Boil until tender
  1. Place the chicken in a pot with the water, half the garlic and ginger, and a pinch of salt.
  2. Simmer covered for 1½–2 hours until tender. Keep the stock.
2 Fry the chicken
  1. Drain the chicken. Heat the oil and fry the pieces until lightly browned.
  2. Add the onions and remaining garlic and ginger and cook until soft.
3 Make the gravy
  1. Add the tomatoes, capsicum and curry powder, cover and cook until the tomatoes melt into a paste.
  2. Pour in 1–2 cups of the reserved stock and simmer for 15 minutes.
4 Finish and serve
  1. Stir in the dhania, check the salt and serve with ugali, chapati or rice.
