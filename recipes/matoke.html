Matoke

Steamed & Stewed Green Bananas

Uganda's national dish: green cooking bananas simmered in a tomato and onion broth until soft and comforting.

  • Total time50 min
  • Serves4
  • DifficultyEasy

The story behind the dish

Matoke (also spelled matooke) is the heart of Ugandan cooking — so central that the same word is used for both the banana and the meal itself. The East African highland banana grows all around the Lake Victoria basin and is harvested green, long before it turns sweet.

Traditionally the peeled bananas are wrapped in banana leaves, steamed for hours and mashed into a soft golden mound. This stewed version is a quicker everyday favourite in Uganda and among the Abagusii of western Kenya, who also claim matoke as a staple.

Ingredients

  • 8 green bananas (matoke), peeled and cut into chunks
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 3 ripe tomatoes, diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 250 g beef, cubed (optional)
  • 2 cups beef stock or water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • A handful of fresh dhania (coriander) to garnish

Serve with: Groundnut (peanut) sauce, beef stew or sukuma wiki.

Cooking Instructions

Tap each step to expand it.

1 Prepare the bananas
  1. Rub a little oil on your hands (the sap is sticky) and peel the green bananas with a knife.
  2. Cut into chunks and keep them in a bowl of cold water so they don't turn brown.
2 Build the stew base
  1. Heat the oil in a heavy pot and fry the onion until soft and golden.
  2. Add the garlic and curry powder, stir for 30 seconds, then add the tomatoes and cook into a thick paste.
  3. If using beef, brown it in the paste for 5 minutes.
3 Simmer until tender
  1. Drain the bananas and add them to the pot with the stock and salt.
  2. Cover and simmer on low heat for 20–25 minutes, until the bananas are fork-tender.
  3. Lightly mash a few pieces against the side of the pot to thicken the sauce.
4 Serve
  1. Garnish with dhania and serve hot, with groundnut sauce or greens on the side.
