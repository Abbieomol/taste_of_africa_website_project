Mchicha wa Nazi

Amaranth Greens in Coconut Milk

Tender amaranth leaves simmered in creamy coconut milk — a quick, comforting Swahili side dish.

  • Total time25 min
  • Serves4
  • DifficultyEasy

The story behind the dish

Mchicha is the Swahili name for amaranth greens and nazi means coconut. Along the coast of Tanzania and Kenya, fresh coconut milk (tui) is squeezed by hand from grated coconut and used to enrich everything from beans to fish to greens.

This dish is a perfect example of everyday coastal cooking: cheap, quick, nourishing and naturally vegetarian. It is usually eaten with ugali or rice, and spinach makes a good substitute where amaranth is hard to find.

Ingredients

  • 2 bunches mchicha (amaranth) or spinach, washed and chopped
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
  • ½ tsp turmeric or curry powder
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 green chilli, sliced (optional)

Serve with: Ugali, wali wa nazi (coconut rice) or grilled fish.

Cooking Instructions

Tap each step to expand it.

1 Soften the aromatics
  1. Heat the oil and fry the onion until soft, then add the garlic and turmeric.
  2. Add the tomatoes and cook until they break down.
2 Wilt the greens
  1. Add the chopped mchicha a handful at a time, stirring until it wilts.
3 Add the coconut milk
  1. Pour in the coconut milk, add salt and chilli, and simmer uncovered for 5–8 minutes.
  2. Stir gently — don't boil hard or the coconut milk can split.
4 Serve
  1. Serve warm with ugali, rice or chapati.
