East Africa

Kenya · Uganda · Tanzania · Rwanda · Ethiopia

East African cooking stretches from the cool highlands of Kenya and Ethiopia to the warm Indian Ocean coast. It is a cuisine of hearty staples, fresh greens and slow-cooked stews, shaped by farming communities, pastoralists and centuries of ocean trade.

Food culture

Meals are made to be shared. A family gathers around a large plate of ugali (stiff maize porridge), torn by hand and used to scoop up stews and greens. On weekends and holidays, nyama choma (grilled meat) and kienyeji chicken bring everyone together, and no Kenyan wedding is complete without a pot of pilau.

Cooking techniques & spices

Inland cooks rely on slow boiling and stewing, while the Swahili coast is famous for cooking in coconut milk. Grilling meat over charcoal — nyama choma — is a national pastime. Trade with Arabia and India brought cardamom, cloves, cinnamon and cumin; Zanzibar is still known as the Spice Island.

Staple foods

  • Ugali (maize), sorghum and millet
  • Matoke (green cooking bananas)
  • Rice, especially aromatic pishori
  • Beans, peas and green grams (ndengu)
  • Leafy greens: sukuma wiki, mchicha, managu
  • Freshwater fish from Lake Victoria
