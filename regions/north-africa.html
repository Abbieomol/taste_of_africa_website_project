North Africa

Morocco · Algeria · Tunisia · Libya · Egypt

North African cuisine sits at the crossroads of Africa, the Mediterranean and the Middle East. Amazigh (Berber), Arab, Andalusian and Ottoman influences have blended into some of the world's most aromatic food.

Food culture

Hospitality is everything: guests are welcomed with sweet mint tea, and Friday lunch in Morocco traditionally means a large shared platter of couscous. Meals are eaten from a communal dish using bread (khobz) as a spoon.

Cooking techniques & spices

The tagine pot slow-braises meat with very little water, while couscous is steamed three times in a couscoussier above a simmering stew. Signature flavours include ras el hanout (a blend of up to 30 spices), saffron, cumin, harissa chilli paste and tangy preserved lemon.

Staple foods

  • Semolina couscous and bread (khobz)
  • Chickpeas, lentils and fava beans
  • Olives and olive oil
  • Lamb, chicken and fish
  • Dates, figs, apricots and almonds
  • Preserved lemons
