West Africa

Nigeria · Ghana · Senegal · Cameroon · Côte d'Ivoire

West Africa is bold, spicy and generous. Its kitchens are built on root crops, rich soups and the deep red colour of palm oil, and its dishes — from jollof rice to suya — are now loved around the world.

Food culture

A typical meal pairs a thick, flavourful soup or stew with a "swallow" — a soft dough of pounded yam, cassava (eba/garri) or plantain, eaten by hand. Food is central to celebrations, and friendly rivalries over who makes the best jollof rice are part of the culture.

Cooking techniques & spices

Flavour starts with red palm oil and a blended base of tomatoes, onions and scotch bonnet peppers. Cooks add umami with ground crayfish, fermented iru (locust beans) and smoked fish. Starches are pounded in a mortar, and meat is often grilled with suya spice (ground peanuts, ginger and chilli).

Staple foods

  • Yam, cassava and plantain
  • Rice (the base of jollof and waakye)
  • Egusi (melon seeds) and groundnuts
  • Black-eyed peas and beans
  • Leafy greens: ugu, bitter leaf, waterleaf
  • Smoked and dried fish
